## The Prompt (copy everything below this line)

---

Create a detailed, client-facing slide deck titled **"Permit Readiness Sprint — What to Expect"** for EntitleFlow, a North Carolina-first land entitlement operations platform. This deck will be sent to prospective clients before a walkthrough call, or presented live during a discovery session. The audience is architecture firm principals, civil engineering PMs, and development team leads who manage active permit review cycles in NC jurisdictions like Greensboro, Raleigh, and Charlotte.

**Tone and voice:** Professional, calm, and precise. No hype, no jargon inflation. Speak like a trusted operations advisor — someone who has seen the mess and knows how to clean it up without drama. Use direct, confident language that respects the audience's intelligence. Never use "revolutionize," "game-changing," or "cutting-edge." Instead use phrases like "cleaner handoffs," "less reactive coordination," and "shared operational clarity."

**Slide structure — build the deck in this exact order:**

**Slide 1 — Title Slide**
Title: "Permit Readiness Sprint"
Subtitle: "A structured workflow audit for teams managing live NC approval cycles"
Include: EntitleFlow branding, "Starting at $3,500" as a clean price anchor, and the tagline: "Clean up the process before the chaos compounds."

**Slide 2 — The Problem We See**
Describe the real pain that architecture and civil firms experience after permit submission: reviewer comments arrive as PDF redlines with no structured way to track, assign, respond to, or resolve them. Teams end up managing this across email threads, personal spreadsheets, scattered PDFs, and tribal memory. The ugliest work happens after the first review cycle, not at the point of submission. Reference the specific pain points: comment ownership gets lost, status visibility requires manual reconstruction, resubmittal packages get assembled under deadline pressure with incomplete responses.

**Slide 3 — Who This Is For**
Three audience profiles pulled from the source documents:
- Architecture firms keeping reviewer comments, discipline owners, and resubmittal prep out of email sprawl
- Civil and site teams tracking jurisdiction requirements, engineering notes, and response cycles with less manual chasing
- Developers and builders who need cleaner status visibility when approvals touch multiple portals, reviewers, and project teams
Add: "Best for firms that need a near-term workflow reset on active NC projects."

**Slide 4 — What the Sprint Delivers**
Three clear deliverables, each with a one-sentence description:
1. **Current State Review** — A structured assessment of how your team currently manages the permit review cycle, scored across five operational domains with a maturity model.
2. **Reviewer Comment & Resubmittal Risk Map** — A quantified risk register that scores where operational breakdowns are most likely to occur in your comment handling and resubmittal coordination workflows.
3. **Recommendation Summary** — An honest platform fit assessment with a rollout readiness score, implementation roadmap, and clear next steps.

**Slide 5 — How It Works (Timeline)**
A clean 10-day timeline broken into five phases:
- Days 1–2: Discovery call and data collection. We walk through your current approval workflow end-to-end, identify active review cycles, and collect sample materials (reviewer comment PDFs, current trackers, team org).
- Days 3–7: Audit execution. We map your workflow against five assessment domains, score operational maturity, identify dependency gaps, and build the risk map. No disruption to your active project work.
- Days 8–9: Deliverable review. A 60-minute presentation session walking through findings, risk priorities, and platform fit assessment.
- Day 10: Final handoff. Polished documents delivered, optional platform demo tied to your specific pain points, and clear next steps documented.

**Slide 6 — The Five Assessment Domains**
List and briefly describe each domain from the Current State Review Framework:
1. Submission & Intake Operations — How permit packages are prepared, submitted, and tracked across jurisdictions
2. Reviewer Comment Handling — How comments arrive, get logged, categorized, assigned, and responded to
3. Resubmittal Coordination — How the team decides what goes into the next package and tracks resolved vs. open items
4. Status Visibility & Reporting — How PMs, principals, and clients get status updates without manual reconstruction
5. Jurisdiction-Specific Workflow Knowledge — Whether local process notes, reviewer patterns, and portal quirks are captured in a shared system or living in someone's head

**Slide 7 — The Maturity Model**
Present the five-level maturity scoring model:
- Level 1 (Reactive): No consistent process. Comments live in email. Status is tribal knowledge.
- Level 2 (Informal): Personal systems exist but aren't shared. The PM coordinates through memory and email.
- Level 3 (Structured): A shared tracker exists. Comment ownership is assigned. Resubmittal prep starts before deadline week.
- Level 4 (Managed): Workflows are documented. Comment handling has a defined process. Clients get regular updates.
- Level 5 (Optimized): Data informs decisions. Jurisdiction-specific playbooks exist. New team members onboard without tribal knowledge.
Note: "Most NC firms we talk to operate between Level 1 and Level 3. The Sprint identifies exactly where you are and what it would take to move up."

**Slide 8 — Risk Mapping Preview**
Explain the risk scoring approach without overwhelming: Likelihood × Impact × Detectability = Risk Priority Number. Show the four risk categories:
- Comment Intake & Triage Risks
- Comment Assignment & Response Risks
- Resubmittal Coordination Risks
- Visibility & Communication Risks
Include one concrete example: "If comments arrive via multiple channels with no single intake point, and unassigned comments aren't detected until deadline crunch, the risk compounds — and the resubmittal quality drops."

**Slide 9 — What You Walk Away With**

## The 3-minute panel / competition pitch

### Hook — 15 seconds

> "Every architecture firm in America has the same broken workflow. They submit a permit, the city sends back a PDF full of red marks, and then someone on that team spends three hours retyping reviewer comments into a spreadsheet before anyone can actually start responding. I'm building the fix."

### Problem — 45 seconds

> "After a permit is submitted, reviewer comments come back as a marked-up PDF. Today, those comments live in five places at once — email threads, Bluebeam markups, somebody's Excel, Slack DMs, and tribal memory. A mid-sized firm is tracking 75 to 250 open comments across their active projects at any given time, and there is no shared system of record.
>
> That's why permit expediter firms in Raleigh and Charlotte charge $25 to $40 an hour — because firms are literally paying humans full-time salaries to solve this coordination problem. That's the market I'm going after."

### Solution — 60 seconds

> "EntitleFlow does two things.
>
> **One** — you drop the redline PDF into our tool, and in under two minutes you get a structured, assignable comment list. No transcription, no retyping, no missed comments. That's the magic moment.
>
> **Two** — once those comments are structured, you assign them to your team, track the response status, and compile the resubmittal package without chasing email threads. That's the operating layer.
>
> The parse is how we earn the demo. The tracking layer is why teams pay."

*(Hold up phone, show QR code on screen or slide)*
> "We have a live tool at entitleflow.com — scan this code, upload your own redline PDF, and you'll see the parse in under 90 seconds. That's my demo."

### Why us / why now — 30 seconds

> "Three reasons this works right now.
>
> **One** — document AI actually works now. The same tech that reads medical forms can read plan review redlines. Two years ago this was impossible. Today it's a weekend.
>
> **Two** — I'm solo, which means my product is priced where a 5-person firm can actually afford it. The enterprise players raised $54 million to chase general contractors. I'm not competing with them. I'm going straight to the firms they ignore.
>
> **Three** — I'm building this in North Carolina first because I live here and I can walk into a firm in Greensboro and sit with the person who's retyping that PDF. That feedback loop is the edge."

### The ask — 30 seconds

> "I'm looking for three things at Startup Week.
>
> **One** — architects, civil engineers, or developers who deal with redline PDFs. I want to put this tool in your hands this week.
>
> **Two** — operators who've seen the coordination pain and want to advise.
>
> **Three** — other founders building in AEC or proptech who want to compare notes.
>
> The QR code is on my card. Let's talk."

### Close — 15 seconds

> "Permit chaos eats developer margins, architect hours, and engineer weekends. EntitleFlow cuts the ugliest part of that cycle in under two minutes. Thank you."

## Frequently asked questions (prep for the floor)

### "How is this different from Bluebeam?"

> "Bluebeam is great for markup — that's where reviewer comments start. But once those markups come back to the architect's team, Bluebeam doesn't help you assign owners, track response status, or compile the resubmittal. That's what we do. Think of us as 'after Bluebeam' — the coordination layer between markup and resubmission."

### "How is this different from PermitFlow?"

> "PermitFlow raised $54 million to help big general contractors submit permits. They're going after multi-state enterprise. We're going after the architect or engineer who has to respond to the reviewer comments *after* submission. Different buyer, different wedge. Their users wish they had our product."

### "Why should I trust a solo founder?"

> "Because solo means my product is priced where you can actually afford it, I can onboard you personally in the first month, and every decision in the product came from a real conversation, not a product-committee compromise. When this scales, price goes up. Right now, I'm cheap and hungry."

### "Is this just for North Carolina firms?"

> "I'm building and testing in NC first because I live here and I can meet my first 20 customers in person. The product works for any AEC firm in the US that gets reviewer redline PDFs back from a municipality. Not a geography play — a GTM play."

### "What's the pricing?"

> "$299 a month per firm. Unlimited projects, unlimited seats. First month includes personal onboarding from me. If you're a 2-person firm, that's fair. If you're a 25-person firm, that's a steal."

### "Where does the AI come in?"

> "Google Cloud Document AI reads the PDF. Vertex AI — Gemini — classifies the comments, flags recurring reviewer patterns, and eventually will draft response letters. Real AI doing real work, not a wrapper around ChatGPT."

### "What if the PDF is garbage?"

> "We handle scanned PDFs, native PDFs, Bluebeam markups, and hand-scribbled reviewer notes. If we can't parse something cleanly, the tool tells you exactly where it got stuck so you can fix that one comment manually — instead of retyping all 25."

### "What's your ask?"

> "Three pilot firms by May 15. Each pilot runs one active permit cycle through EntitleFlow. I want feedback, case studies, and logos. Pricing is free for the pilot period."

## Do's and Don'ts on the floor

**Do:**
- Lead with the concrete 2-minute parse time
- Show the tool on your phone if anyone asks
- Ask about their current workflow before pitching solutions
- Collect email + company + role on every useful conversation
- Take notes on objections — they become next week's product priorities

**Don't:**
- Start with the AI — start with the pain
- Mention the full product roadmap — you're selling one wedge this week
- Pitch investors unless they ask — you're selling pilots, not equity
- Get lost in the NC-first story — lead with the universal pain first, then mention NC as your beachhead
- Apologize for anything — the product is real, the demo works, the pain is clear

# EntitleFlow Brand Voice Guide

**Version:** 1.0 | **Last Updated:** March 27, 2026 | **Owner:** Jene Maybury

---

## Brand Personality

If EntitleFlow were a person, it would be a sharp, calm operations lead who has worked inside NC permitting offices and understands the pain of chasing reviewer comments across PDFs, emails, and portals. They speak with quiet authority, never oversell, and always ground their advice in the real workflow — not theory. They respect your time, hate busywork, and believe regional firms deserve tools built for how they actually work.

---

## Voice Attributes (5 Pillars)

### 1. Grounded

- **We are:** Rooted in real NC jurisdiction workflows, specific to actual pain points, never abstract
- **We are not:** Vague, buzzword-heavy, or making promises that sound like every other SaaS homepage
- **This sounds like:** "Built around actual Greensboro, Raleigh, and Charlotte review cycles — not a generic template."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "Our AI-powered platform revolutionizes the permitting space with cutting-edge technology."

### 2. Operator-First

- **We are:** Speaking to the people doing the work — PMs, discipline leads, civil teams in the field
- **We are not:** Speaking at executives from a stage or pitching to investors
- **This sounds like:** "Your reviewer comments shouldn't live in five different email threads."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "Unlock enterprise-grade workflow orchestration across your portfolio."

### 3. Calm Confidence

- **We are:** Assured without being loud, showing strength through specificity and depth
- **We are not:** Hype-driven, aggressive, or using urgency as a manipulation tactic
- **This sounds like:** "We started in North Carolina because that is where we know the work best."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "DON'T MISS OUT! The permit management revolution is HERE!"

### 4. Regionally Sharp

- **We are:** Proudly NC-first, treating regional focus as a competitive advantage, not a limitation
- **We are not:** Apologetic about our market scope or pretending to be a national platform before we earn it
- **This sounds like:** "North Carolina-specific workflow depth instead of a generic national template."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "We serve all 50 states and every municipality in the country."

### 5. Anti-Bloat

- **We are:** Clear, lean, no unnecessary features, no enterprise theater
- **We are not:** Minimalist to the point of being unhelpful — we're comprehensive where it counts
- **This sounds like:** "Control without the overhead. Regional teams don't need another enterprise implementation."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "Our fully customizable enterprise-scale solution adapts to any organization size."

## Core Messaging Pillars (Priority Order)

### Pillar 1: The Post-Submission Problem
The ugliest work in land entitlement happens *after* submission — reviewer comments, resubmittals, and the coordination chaos between. That is where EntitleFlow lives.

### Pillar 2: NC-First Depth
We are not a national platform with a North Carolina checkbox. We are built from NC jurisdiction research outward — Greensboro, Raleigh, Charlotte workflows are in our DNA.

### Pillar 3: Operational Clarity for Regional Teams
Architecture firms, civil teams, and developers deserve one live view of where approvals stand without buying into bloated software that was designed for someone else.

### Pillar 4: Founder-Led, Not Self-Serve Guesswork
Our launch motion starts with guided walkthroughs and workflow audits — real understanding of your operations before we hand you a login.

### Pillar 5: AI That Serves the Workflow
FlowE AI agents handle document parsing, comment classification, and response suggestions — but the human operator stays in control of every decision.

## Audience Profiles

### Primary: NC Architecture & Civil Firm PMs
Project managers and discipline leads at 5-50 person firms running repeat permitting workflows in Greensboro, Raleigh, Charlotte, and surrounding jurisdictions. They are drowning in PDFs, email chains, and ad-hoc trackers. They want control without complexity.

### Secondary: Developers & Property Managers
Builder-developers and property managers who coordinate across multiple consultants and need cleaner visibility into where approvals stand without getting pulled into the day-to-day workflow detail.

### Tertiary: Firm Principals & Leadership
Need status visibility for client reporting and business development without disrupting the team's operating rhythm.

## Style Rules

### Grammar & Mechanics
- **Oxford comma:** Yes ("comments, owners, and resubmittals")
- **Headings:** Sentence case ("How it works" not "How It Works")
- **Contractions:** Use them ("we're", "don't", "isn't") — we talk like people
- **Numbers:** Spell out one through nine, numerals for 10+
- **Percent:** Use the symbol (50%, not "fifty percent")
- **Dates:** Month DD, YYYY ("March 27, 2026")
- **Em dashes:** No spaces ("comments—and the responses that follow")

### Formatting
- Bold for emphasis, italic for introducing terms
- Descriptive link text always (never "click here")
- Exclamation marks: sparingly, max one per piece of content
- ALL CAPS: never
- Emoji: only on social platforms, and only when it adds clarity

## Terminology Guide

### Preferred Terms

| Use This | Not This | Why |
|----------|----------|-----|
| Reviewer comments | Redline comments, markups | Our umbrella term for all feedback from jurisdiction reviewers |
| Resubmittal | Re-submission, resubmission | Industry-standard NC term |
| Approval workflow | Permitting pipeline | "Pipeline" sounds like sales; "workflow" sounds like operations |
| Jurisdiction | Municipality, city | More precise and inclusive of county-level entities |
| Discipline lead | Department head | Matches how architecture/civil firms actually talk |
| Operational clarity | Visibility, transparency | Our differentiated framing — implies action, not just seeing |
| Workflow audit | Discovery call, consultation | Reflects what we actually do in the Permit Readiness Sprint |
| EntitleFlow | Entitle Flow, entitleflow | One word, capital E capital F |
| FlowE | Flow-E, Flowy | Our AI agent brand — one word, capital F capital E |

### Terms to Avoid

| Avoid | Why | Use Instead |
|-------|-----|-------------|
| Revolutionize / Disrupt | Overpromises, cliche | "Designed to replace..." or "Built to clean up..." |
| Enterprise-grade | We serve regional firms, not enterprises | "Built for regional teams" |
| Seamless / Frictionless | No software is frictionless | "Cleaner" or "more controlled" |
| AI-powered (as lead) | AI is a feature, not the value prop | Lead with the workflow outcome, mention AI as how |
| One-stop shop | Generic and meaningless | Be specific about what we consolidate |
| Best-in-class | Empty claim | Show the specific advantage instead |
| Synergy | Just... no | Describe the actual coordination benefit |

## Competitor Language Rules

- Never disparage competitors by name
- Frame against "manual processes" and "generic national platforms" — the real competition
- Use "EntitleFlow" framing: "NC-first" vs. "national template", "post-submission" vs. "submission-focused"
- Do not adopt terminology competitors have coined — use our own vocabulary
